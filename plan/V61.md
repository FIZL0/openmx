# V61 — the rider holds the bike the way the original does: its limb weights and its solve, proved by code

User, 2026-10-23: "the new tricks work but it looks like the character doesnt always grab the bike
properly", then "make it accurate to the game, the character looking and moving right should be a high
priority … we should do it by code not by eye, keep it versitile", and "then we can remove our scuffed
version after it's fixed".

That overrides AGENT.md's "rendering: match by eye" for this one area. How the rider's limbs hold the
bike is ported from the original and proved against it in code, like the simulation. It does not have
to be bit-exact through `ps2-float`, since it only reaches the picture, but it is the original's
arithmetic, checked to a stated tolerance.

## What the port does today, and why it is wrong

`crates/game-render/src/rider.rs`'s `hold_on` (V10, 2026-10-17) pulls each hand and foot onto the
chassis' grips and pegs every frame. Two of its pieces are the port's own, and V10's journal
(`research/journal/2026-10-17-v10-rider-lod-and-ik/`) says so:

- **The weight**: `holding()`, `HOLD_WITHIN` (1 ft) and `LET_GO_AT` (2 ft) decide how hard each limb
  holds on from how far the clip already put it. This was fitted to MX Unleashed's own gesture clips.
  - A hand between 1 and 2 ft off its grip is half-pulled. That is the "doesn't grab properly" the
    user sees.
  - MX vs. ATV's clips (MD21) were made against another bike, so they fall into that band more often.
- **The solve**: `aim` is a law-of-cosines hinge plus one swing over shoulder, elbow and hand. The
  original's is closed-form VU0 quaternion code. It also has a clavicle and a wrist in the chain, which
  the port does not, so some arms end short of the grip (`onehandindianair`, 2.9 inches).

## What is already known (V10, plus a look on 2026-10-23)

- Four limb controllers of 0x110 bytes are built in the rider setup: the function that looks up
  `Dummy_Foot_L/R` and `Dummy_Hand_L/R` by name (V10's journal has its address).
- The controller initialiser stores three things: the target slot at `+0x60`, a pointer from the rider
  at `+0x64` (the `+0x530` V10 read as a lean), and the **bone object** at `+0x68`.
- The solve opens by copying `*(controller + 0x68) + 0x10` into `controller + 0x100`. So **the weight
  is a field of the bone object**, re-read every frame. The bone object is what the animation system
  poses.
- The likeliest writer is therefore the animation blender. It may even be a channel or flag the clips
  carry, which would make the weight come with every clip, MX vs. ATV's included. That is a
  hypothesis: prove it, don't assume it.
- The solve is reached only through a function-pointer table, so a static search for callers finds
  nothing. That is where V10 stopped. Tools that get past it:
  - `./kit xref` on the table;
  - a search for stores to `+0x10` of the bone type;
  - a live read of the four bones' `+0x10` over a playback.

## What to do (split into sub-tasks if it is more than one session)

1. **Find the weight's writer.** Read the four bone objects' `+0x10` every tick over a trick-filled
   playback with `./kit record`, and find the code that stores it (static search for the store, or
   `./kit xref`). Say in the journal what decides it: a clip channel, a per-trick table, the rider
   state, or the vehicle.
2. **Port the weight** so it is computed as the original computes it, for any clip, any skeleton and
   any pack. Nothing may be fitted to MX Unleashed's clips: a clip from MX vs. ATV or a later pack
   must get its weight by the same rule.
3. **Port the solve** (the VU0 function V10 names), clavicle and wrist included, for every skeleton
   the rider sets bind to.
4. **Remove the port's own versions** once 2 and 3 are proved: `holding`, `HOLD_WITHIN`, `LET_GO_AT`
   and `aim`, and V10's tests that only check them against themselves.
5. Then S111 (the riding ragdoll's request) can build on this. The ragdoll is what the original
   actually draws on the bike.

## Proof (code, not eyes)

- **Oracle**: run the original's solve on the EE oracle over 10,000 or more generated limb poses and
  targets, against the port's solve, with a stated tolerance.
- **Weight**: the port's per-limb weight against the captured `+0x10` values, tick for tick, over a
  playback with gestures in it (S109's animandpump, or C8's when it exists).
- **The whole hold**: the four limbs' end positions against the original's posed bones read from a
  capture over the same playback. This is the test the user means by "looking right".
- Every MXU disc clip and every clip of the MX vs. ATV pack goes through the ported weight and solve
  without a limb left unsolved for lack of a joint. The test skips, and says so, when there is no pack.
