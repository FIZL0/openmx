- Found and fixed two things that were not the original's, both in the sim:
  1. **The bottom-out shove moves the body** (`wheel.rs`, `ride.rs`). This was the snap. When a
     suspension passes its stop, the original's wheel step computes
     `push = contact normal × (comp − max) × 0.9` and hands it to the transform call (0x001b2770)
     as its third argument, in a2. With no parent frame, that call adds the push to the
     transform's origin and rebuilds. The decompiler drops a2, and the port had kept the push as
     "never read again". On the big drop (2751) the original moves the body up by 0.49 in that one
     call. Without the shove the port's body sank through and its springs threw it back up, so
     it rebounded 1 to 2.6 higher than the original over the next ticks. That is the snap the user
     feels. Now `Call::TransformSync(push)` carries the push, and `ride.rs`'s `answer` adds it to the
     node's origin, which the integrator then reads. The wheel oracle test records the call's a2 and
     holds the port's push to it bit for bit (`tests/wheel.rs`, the "bottomed out with the push"
     coverage).
  2. **The frame ends with the chassis quaternion re-read off the node** (`ride.rs`, step 5). In
     `bike_vehicle_update`, after the ground step, stunt_state_pack stores `mat4_to_quat` of the
     node's world rows, and stunt_clocks copies it back into the chassis quaternion. The
     no-hander's roll (and anything else that turned the node after the integrator) is then in the
     body for the next frame's measure, not a frame late. The new test
     `the_vehicle_update_leaves_the_chassis_its_nodes_quaternion` runs the original's whole update on
     all seven states, and holds the chassis quaternion to the port's `mat4_to_quat` of the node it
     leaves, bit for bit.
- Found the pad alignment for these captures: row `k`'s tick is driven by `tape.frames[k − 2]`, not

## What the original does

- `./kit fn 0x0025bc70` (wheel_tire_forces), the bottom-out branch:
  `push = n(+0x130) × (comp − max(+0x48)) × 0.9` is stored to a stack slot. That slot's address goes
  in a2 to `./kit fn 0x001b2770`. With relative_to 0 and no parent, that is `./kit fn 0x001b2730`:
  `+0x70 += v.x; +0x74 += v.y; +0x78 += v.z`, then the rebuild copies the local origin into the world
  one. The velocity, acceleration and force pushes after it are what the port already had.
- `./kit fn 0x002520d0` (bike_vehicle_update), in order:
  1. the ground step 0x00257a00;
  2. bike_air_state 0x00251d20;
  3. 0x00133500;
  4. stunt_idle 0x00258cc0;
  5. stunt_state_pack 0x00258d50, which writes +0x830 = mat4_to_quat(node +0x80), +0x840 = chassis
     velocity and +0x850 = chassis +0x280;
  6. stunt_clocks 0x002593e0 with a1 = 0, which copies +0x830 → chassis +0x1a0, +0x840 → +0x260 and
     +0x8b4 → +0x294;
  7. the ride state machine.

## How it was proved

- **The push:** the function oracle in `tests/wheel.rs` (`wheel_tire_forces` over its generated and
  state inputs). The transform call's hook now records a2's vector, and the port's `TransformSync(push)`
  matches it to the bit.
- **The copy-back:** `the_vehicle_update_leaves_the_chassis_its_nodes_quaternion` runs the original's
  whole vehicle update on the seven S67 RAM images, and holds the chassis quaternion to the node's,
  bit for bit (7 states run).
- **The landings:** `each_captured_landing_rides_like_the_original`, 7 landings from save states with
  the original's pad, compared tick by tick against two PINE captures. It is held to a bound, not to
  the bit; see below.

## Not verified / not 1:1

- **The landings are not bit-exact.** They are held to a bound. Two playbacks of the same recording
  already part in the last bits (run2 against landings over the same frames). The capture reads its
  rows at three phases of a frame: about one row in four is stale, and the row after can hold the tick
  it missed. And the cofg drifts by about 1e-3 over a few ticks of flight even before contact.
- **The nose-first drop (3452 → touchdown 3459) parts at the touchdown tick.** The front lands at 48 a
  second straight down. On that one tick the velocity comes out −22.35 against −22.63 in x, the spin
  −1.71 against −1.78, and the front force 6003.7 against 5944.3. The lower speed then shifts the port
  into first gear at 3462, two ticks before the original's 3464, and the rear lands at 3464 with comp
  0.726 against 0.694. Not found. Pinning the port's cofg, velocity, quaternion and spin to the
  original's 3458 row changed nothing, because the frame rebuilds the body from its node. A fair test
  needs a save state at 3458, which was not taken. It may be only the 1e-3 drift amplified by the
  hardest impact in the capture, or something on the front-only bottom-out path. Follow-up filed.
- **The f−2 pad lag is read off the result,** not off the recorder. It is the alignment at which the
  2751 touchdown agrees to 1e-6, and it matches recording2's. The recorder's polling was not traced
  again here.
- **The flick's sample ring and the d-pad axes are not seeded** from the state (`seed_held`).
  animandpump rides the stick, and no landing parts where the flick would show.
- **NominalScalar** (`+0xc8`, `SHARE_SCALE`) is 1.0 in every state read. No `.phy` key was found that
  writes it.
- **The bike class is race500p.phy.** The state's power curve is that file's to the bit (asserted).
  The other 500 classes differ only in tires and air control, which the curve does not tell apart.
- **The translate's parent-frame branch is not ported.** No state had a parent on the chassis node.
- **The port's own scenarios were re-pinned, not re-derived.** The MD16/MD25 360s, the 500% engine
  and the hands-off flight changed because the sim is now closer to the original, but nothing says
  their new numbers are what MX vs. ATV or a stronger engine "should" give.
- **The shell is ruled out by reading,** plus the test's drawn/aim lanes. The bit-exact camera is
  proved elsewhere (tests/camera.rs), not re-run on these landings, and nothing here checks pixels on
  screen.
